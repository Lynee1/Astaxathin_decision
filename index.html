What it answers

Is it time to move to red?

More days in green build biomass but delay induction and tie up reactors. The planner scores every possible transfer day by output over the whole cycle, not by today's cell density.

Are more days in red worth it?

Astaxanthin keeps rising, but each extra day holds the reactor. The best harvest day is where the daily gain falls below the batch average, shown with a likely range.

Can I trust the advice when conditions change?

Heatwaves, dull weeks, missed measurements, equipment faults or contamination switch the system to conservative advice, or hand the decision back to your operators.

Pilot programme

We start in shadow mode alongside your current practice, then calibrate and test before any change to production. You decide at each step whether to continue.

  1. Weeks 0–2Data onboardingGather your SOP and batch records and map them to one template.
  2. Weeks 2–6Shadow modeRun two or three batches as usual while the system makes recommendations in parallel.
  3. Weeks 6–10CalibrationFit the model to your batches and report its accuracy on a batch it has not seen.
  4. Weeks 10–16Transfer-timing trialSplit one seed culture across three or four transfer days to confirm the model ranks them correctly.
  5. Week 16 onwardsControlled adoptionFollow recommendations when safeguards are clear; your operators decide otherwise.

How we judge success

Whole-cycle productivityAstaxanthin per reactor-day, compared with your current schedule.
Batch consistencyLess variation in harvest timing and pigment content between batches.
Cost per kilogramProduction cost of saleable astaxanthin at your volumes and prices.

What we need from you

  • One strain, your current SOP, and records from two or three recent batches
  • For each batch: inoculation, transfer and harvest dates, working volume and any dilution at transfer
  • Every one or two days: dry-weight biomass, or optical density calibrated to dry weight
  • Every two or three days: astaxanthin content, noting HPLC or spectrophotometry
  • Daily: mean and maximum temperature, light (PAR readings or weather irradiance) and microscopy notes
  • Events: shutdowns, feeding, contamination treatment and equipment faults, with dates
